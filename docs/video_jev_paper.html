Video Jev: one calibrated head for typed questions about video and sound

Ask a video a typed question: yes or no, pick one, score it, or "when?". Get back a probability for every possible answer. Many questions in one pass.

Backbone: Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3BWeights: huggingface.co/sp1d5r/video-jevLicence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
Abstract

We add a LoRA adapter and a typed answer head to Qwen3-Omni, an open model that reads video frames and sound on one timeline. Questions are written as Jev records: yes/no, choice, ordered score, and a new moment type for "when" questions, where each second of video is an answer option scored on its own video and audio tokens. One forward pass returns a calibrated probability for every answer of every question. A single joint model, trained on five public tasks with randomly reworded questions, beats the untrained backbone on every task. On official test splits it reaches R1@0.5 0.679 on QVHighlights (untrained 0.528, Moment-DETR 0.539), 0.776 HIT@1 on FunQA moments, 0.743 mean Spearman on VideoFeedback (VideoScore 0.771) and 0.996 AUC on real-or-AI. It loses nothing on question wordings it never saw, and answers a yes/no question it was never trained on with AUC 0.970 (untrained 0.873). A task-specific model trained the same way reaches 0.775 on VideoFeedback, and 0.709 on SnapUGC engagement, level with the 2025 challenge winner's four-model ensemble. We release the joint model for non-commercial use.

1. Introduction

Jev is a format for decisions. A record holds some state (context) and a set of typed questions, each with its allowed answers. A Jev model returns a probability for each answer, and code acts on those numbers: thresholds, rankings, or sending unsure cases to a person. That only works if the probabilities are calibrated, so that "84%" is right about 84% of the time.

Clef, the existing Jev model, reads text and images. Video models today mostly answer in free text, report accuracy only, and give timestamps as generated text with no confidence. We ask whether one head on an open audio-visual model can answer typed questions about raw video, including "when" questions, with probabilities that can be trusted.

Contributions.

  • A moment question type that reuses the video's own tokens as answer options, so "when" questions need no detector and no timestamp decoding, and return a probability per second.
  • One joint model for score, yes/no, choice and moment questions across five public tasks, trained with reworded questions, and tested on wordings and a question it never saw.
  • Test-split results against the untrained backbone and published task-specific systems, with calibration reported for every question type.
  • A released adapter and head, with a standalone inference script.

2. Method

2.1 Questions

TypeAnswerExample
noulP(yes), P(no)"Can you hear someone laughing?" "Is this AI-generated?"
choiceP per named option"Which sound is most prominent: laughing, giggling, cheering, speech...?"
scoreP per ordered level"How good is the visual quality: bad, average, good, perfect?"
momentP per second"When is the funny moment?" "When does this happen: a man opens a fridge?"

2.2 Typed head

The record is written as text after the video and audio tokens, with each question's options listed in the prompt. We use the joint schema head from Clef: it reads the backbone's final hidden states over each option's tokens and gives that option one logit. A softmax over a question's options gives its probabilities. All questions in a record are answered in one forward pass.

2.3 Moment questions

Qwen3-Omni places video tokens in blocks of two frames, with each block's audio tokens next to it. For a moment question, each time bin is an option, and its tokens are that bin's own video and audio tokens. The head is unchanged; it scores every bin against the question. The answer is a distribution over seconds, from which we take the top moment, a span around the peak, or the top few candidates.

2.4 Training

  • Trainable: LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128) on the language model's attention projections, plus the head in fp32. The rest of Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B is frozen in bf16.
  • Loss: cross-entropy on soft targets (label smoothing 0.05) + Brier score + an ordinal earth mover's distance for score and moment questions. Moment targets weight each bin by its overlap with the answer window, or by human highlight scores where they exist.
  • Reworded questions: every question has five wordings. Training draws one of the first three at random each time a clip is seen; the last two are kept for testing.
  • Recipe: LoRA learning rate 3e-5, head 1e-4, 1 epoch, warmup then cosine, 8-clip gradient accumulation. Chosen on SnapUGC and reused unchanged for every task.

Metrics. Scores: Spearman and Pearson correlation of the expected level with the human score. Yes/no: AUC. Choice: accuracy. Moments: HIT@1 (the top second is inside the answer, or rated "very good" in QVHighlights), R1@0.5 and R1@0.7 (the span of bins at 50% or more of the peak overlaps the answer by that IoU), mIoU and mAP. Calibration: Brier score and expected calibration error (ECE, 10 bins).

Baselines. (1) The same Qwen3-Omni, untrained, asked each question as text: we read the probability of each answer word ("1" to "4", "yes"/"no", option letters); for moments it writes a start and end time. (2) Single-task models trained with the same recipe on one dataset each. (3) Published task-specific systems on the same split.

Protocol. All choices are made on validation data. Each final model is scored once on each test split. Every number is from one training run.

Single-task models use all of their dataset; the joint model uses 10,000 of 35,041 VideoFeedback clips. Moment-DETR numbers are its published single-run val result (QVHighlights supplement, 10 queries).

  • The joint model beats the untrained backbone on every task, by 0.09 to 0.40.
  • Joint training costs almost nothing where data is equal: FunQA +0.009, VGGSound -0.008. VideoFeedback is 0.032 lower, with a third of the data.

  • Real-or-AI trained on VideoFeedback carries over to GenVideo's generators (0.942).

4.2 AI-video quality per question

  • The VideoFeedback-only model is level with VideoScore (0.775 against 0.771): ahead on temporal consistency, text alignment and factual consistency, behind on visual quality and dynamic degree.
  • The joint model trails it most on text alignment (0.599 against 0.660).
  • Untrained, the backbone reaches 0.340, already above GPT-4o's published 0.230.

The laughing question is a new yes/no question about clips seen only with the 8-way "which sound" question. Its labels come from the class names (adult laughter, giggling, baby laughter against speech, crowd and cheering). The model answers it far better than the untrained backbone, so the head follows the question, not only the task. We also held out FunQA's creative moments as an unseen question family, but that test is void: every answer window in that subset starts within the first 3 seconds, so it measures position, not content.

The training loss alone, with no temperature scaling, brings score questions from badly calibrated to ECE under 0.05. VGGSound is the exception: its official test is harder than its val for every model (the untrained backbone drops from 0.66 to 0.50 accuracy), and calibration suffers with it.

4.5 Does audio help?

  • When the answer is in the sound (VGGSound), audio adds 6.4 points and cuts ECE from 0.058 to 0.021.
  • When it is visual (FunQA pranks and tricks), audio adds little.
  • For engagement, the gain is inside the noise.

5. Analysis

  • Dataset shortcuts. In GenVideo every real clip is 10 s at 640x360 and every generated clip is 7.2 s or shorter, and our first record put the duration in the state. A first model and a CLIP baseline both reached 0.999. All real-or-AI clips now use the first 1.5 s, a square crop and 256 px, with no duration. A baseline near 1.0 is a warning, not a result.
  • Learning rate decides stability. A LoRA rate near 1e-4 held for 400 or more steps collapsed twice; 3e-5 trained cleanly on every task. Short sweeps cannot show this failure.
  • Data volume matters more than task mixing. The joint model matches single-task models where it sees the same data, and trails only where it sees less.
  • Text-query moments need data. With 2,000 training queries the model was below the untrained backbone (R1@0.5 0.26 on val); with all 6,862 it was well above (0.68 on test).
  • Benchmarks can be void. FunQA's creative subset answers are all at the clip start, so a position prior solves it.

7. Limitations

  • One seed per result; no run-to-run spread yet.
  • QVHighlights ranking: HIT@1 and mAP are well below dedicated models.
  • Real-or-AI uses generators from 2023 and 2024. It says nothing about modern generators.
  • Long video loses accuracy with length; the test videos are synthetic splices.
  • Short inputs: 60 s per forward pass by default, 150 s at most in training.
  • Engagement is a separate model on our own split, and is not released.
  • Licences: research and non-commercial use only.
