Video Jev: one calibrated head for typed questions about video and sound

Ask a video a typed question: yes or no, pick one, score it, or "when?". Get back a probability for every possible answer. Many questions in one pass.

Backbone: Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (Apache 2.0)Compute: Modal, 1 × H200Spend to date: $337 billedWeights: huggingface.co/sp1d5r/video-jevCode: multimodal-jev
Summary
  • What it is. We add a small trained head to an open audio-visual model, Qwen3-Omni. The head reads questions written in Jev form and returns a calibrated probability for every allowed answer. One model, one forward pass, many questions.
  • One model for everything. A single set of weights (G4) answers score, yes/no, choice and moment questions across five tasks, follows wordings it never saw, and answers a yes/no question it was never trained on (AUC 0.970 against 0.873 untrained).
  • Engagement (SnapUGC): 0.709 on held-out test, level with the 2025 challenge winner's 4-model ensemble (0.710, different split).
  • AI-video quality (VideoFeedback): 0.775 average Spearman on the official test split, against VideoScore's 0.771. Ahead on 3 of 5 questions, from a general recipe with no task tuning.
  • "When" questions: a new moment type, with no new parts, finds the funny moment in FunQA 81% of the time, against 53% for the best blind guess.
  • Sound: on "which sound is this?", audio adds 6.4 points and cuts calibration error by 64%.
  • Text-query moments: QVHighlights R1@0.5 0.679 on its val split, against 0.528 untrained and 0.539 for Moment-DETR.
  • What does not work yet. Span ranking (mAP) on QVHighlights; modern AI-video detection; long videos lose accuracy with length (13% at 10 minutes, 15 times chance).
  • Released. The joint model is on Hugging Face (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), trained only on datasets with a stated licence. $87 of the $150 was spent.

1. What we are building

Jev is a format for decisions. A record holds some state (context) and a set of typed questions. Each question lists its allowed answers. A Jev model returns a probability for each answer, and code acts on those numbers.

Clef, the existing Jev model, reads text and images. Video Jev reads raw video with its sound. The goal is a general head: write a new question in Jev form, and the model answers it with a probability you can trust.

Why probabilities, and why calibration

A model that says "yes" is hard to build on. A model that says "yes, 84%" lets code set thresholds, rank clips, and send unsure cases to a person. That only works if 84% means right about 84% of the time. We measure this with Brier score (squared error of the probabilities) and ECE (expected calibration error: the gap between stated confidence and actual accuracy). Most video models report accuracy only.

2. How it works

The head

The answer options are written in the prompt. The head reads the model's hidden states over each option's tokens and gives that option one logit. A softmax over a question's options gives its probabilities. All questions in a record are answered in one forward pass, so ten questions cost about the same as one.

The moment trick

Qwen3-Omni lays video and audio tokens on one timeline, in 2-frame blocks. For a "when" question, each time bin is an option. The option's tokens are that bin's own video and audio tokens. The same head scores every bin, so the answer is a probability for every second. This needs no detector and no generated timestamps, and it can report the top few moments and how sure it is.

Training

  • Trainable: LoRA (rank 64) on the language model's attention, plus the head. About 0.2% of parameters.
  • Loss: cross-entropy with soft targets, plus Brier score, plus an ordinal distance (EMD) for score and moment questions. Calibration comes from this loss; no step after training is needed.
  • Recipe: LoRA learning rate 3e-5, head 1e-4, 1 epoch, cosine schedule, 8-clip gradient accumulation. Found on SnapUGC and reused unchanged on every new task.
  • Splits: official test sets where they exist, scored once per final model. Choices are made on val only.

3.2 AI-video quality: VideoFeedback

  • Official test split, 760 clips, scored once: 0.775 against VideoScore's 0.771.
  • Ahead on temporal consistency, text alignment and factual consistency.

3.4 Sound: does audio help?

  • VGGSound (8 people-sound classes: laughing, giggling, baby laughter, cheering, crowd, woman, man and child speech): audio adds 6.4 points, and ECE falls from 0.058 to 0.021. This is the clean audio result.
  • FunQA: no clear gain. Its moments are visual: pranks, tricks, magic.
  • SnapUGC: a small gain inside the noise.
  • So the model uses sound when the answer is in the sound.

3.5 Real or AI

On GenVideo (10 generators from 2023 to 2024) and across to VideoFeedback's sources, our model ties a CLIP baseline (AUC 0.929 against 0.934). These older generators are easy to spot from single frames. A useful test needs modern generators (Sora or Veo class), and no open labelled set of them exists yet.

What surprised us

  • The recipe did not need retuning. Settings found on engagement worked on quality, moments, sound and real-or-AI.
  • Calibration is close to free. ECE stayed between 0.02 and 0.06 on every trained task, with no temperature scaling. Untrained, it was 0.18 to 0.56.
  • Datasets have traps. One real-or-AI set could be solved from clip length alone. A baseline at 0.999 is a warning, not a result.

How we test "general"

  • New wordings. Each question is trained with several paraphrases, and tested with paraphrases it never saw.
  • New question families. Hold one out completely. For example, train on funny and magic moments, then ask for the creative moment. Or train four VideoFeedback questions, then ask the fifth.
  • If the model beats its untrained self on these, the head generalises beyond its training questions. That is what makes it a general Jev head, not five task models in one file.
Still not done
  • More seeds (about $140 for two more), so headline numbers come from one run each.
  • Better span ranking on QVHighlights (mAP).
  • Detection of modern AI video and sports goal timing: these need data we do not have.
  • Long video beyond a small test: accuracy falls with length.
  • Engagement in the release: SnapUGC has no stated licence.
  • Commercial use. FunQA is CC BY-NC-SA, so the release is non-commercial.

7. Use cases

Ready means the question type is trained and measured. Partly means one part is shown and the rest needs data. Each use case is a Jev record: you write the questions and get probabilities back.

Clip finder for creators ready

When is the funniest moment? Will viewers keep watching? Find the best seconds of a video. Engagement scoring works too (section 3.1), but is not in the release because SnapUGC has no stated licence. This follows directly from the clipping-for-distribution work.

Reviewer for AI video ready

Visual quality 1 to 4? Does it match the prompt? Does the physics hold? Score every generation, drop bad takes, and give a generator a reward signal (the generate-then-review loop).

Sound-aware tagging ready

Is that laughing, cheering or speech? Did this get a laugh? Tag clips by what you hear, not just what you see, with a probability per tag.

Triage with a human in the loop ready

Because the probabilities are calibrated, auto-accept above 95%, auto-reject below 5%, and send the rest to a person. A plain classifier cannot set these thresholds safely.

Dataset labelling for research ready

Label a large video set with several typed questions in one pass each. Use the uncertain clips for human labelling next (active learning).

Find a moment by text ready

When does the woman open the door? Search inside videos up to a few minutes. R1@0.5 0.679 on QVHighlights; longer videos lose accuracy.

Sports and live events partly

When does the goal go in? Did the crowd cheer? The moment and sound parts both work. It needs labelled match data (SoccerNet needs a signed agreement).

Spotting modern AI video not yet

Was this AI-generated? Works on old generators, but so does CLIP. Modern generators need a new labelled set.

9. Limitations

  • One seed per result. We do not yet know the run-to-run spread.
  • Engagement is not in the release. SnapUGC has no stated licence; its 0.709 is a separate model.
  • SnapUGC split differs from the challenge. The official test labels are withheld.
  • Only 32% of SnapUGC videos are still online, and the deleted ones skew to low engagement.
  • Short clips. Up to 16 s for engagement and 150 s for QVHighlights. Long video is untested.
  • Real-or-AI uses old generators and ties CLIP.
  • Licences: research and non-commercial use only. We release weights, never the videos.
